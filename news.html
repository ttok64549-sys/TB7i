Announcement

We're Hiring: 3D Environment Artist

Help us build Rawalpindi. Remote-friendly, Unity 3D pipeline. Details on the careers page.

The core combat loop is now fully playable in-engine. Over the past weeks we've implemented a complete 3D shooting framework — weapon handling, recoil patterns, spread, hit detection and damage falloff.

Melee defence is also in: a shove/strike system that lets you create space when a zombie gets too close. Stamina ties both systems together, so you can't simply sprint and swing forever.

Next up: enemy reaction animations, weapon variety and the first pass on sound design.

Setting a horror game in Rawalpindi means the environment has to feel lived-in. We spent weeks studying reference — narrow lanes, tangled wiring, shopfronts, signage and the specific way light falls between buildings at dusk.

Our approach: block out the real street layout first, then layer narrative decay on top. Cars abandoned mid-turn, barricades, scattered belongings — the story of the collapse should be readable from the geometry alone.

Lighting is doing the heavy lifting. Warm sodium lamps against cold moonlight, thick fog for draw-distance occlusion, and deep shadows in every alley.

Zombies are only scary if they feel unpredictable. We built our AI on behaviour trees with layered perception — sight cones, sound radius and a simple "memory" of where you were last detected.

Swarm logic allows groups to spread, flank and converge. Some infected are slow and numerous; others are fast and solitary. Audio cues are your only reliable warning.

The result: every encounter forces a decision — fight, hide, or run.

Today we're proud to officially announce Sector Zero — an original 3D zombie-apocalypse survival game set in a devastated Rawalpindi, Pakistan.

Developed in Unity 3D by The Black Studio, Sector Zero focuses on survival, exploration, combat, atmosphere and storytelling — combining intense zombie horror with an authentic South Asian urban setting.
